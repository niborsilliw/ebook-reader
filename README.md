# 📖 Nancy: V1.0 Tester Guide

**The Link:** https://ebook-reader-flame.vercel.app

**The Concept:** Nancy is a reader first. The page is the product: classic typography, quiet margins, no clutter. On larger screens, it opens as a two-page spread. Beneath the page sits a conversation and a research assistant.

---

### 🚀 How to get started
1. Open the link and sign in with your email (check spam for the magic link).
2. Upload an EPUB (you can download free ones from Project Gutenberg).
3. Click the book to open it.

---

### 🧪 What to test

**1. The Calm Page**
*   Turn pages using the Left/Right Arrow keys (desktop) or by swiping/tapping the margins (mobile).
*   Move your mouse to the top-left corner (or tap the screen on mobile) to reveal the Bookshelf icon (Library/Contents) and the Conversation Bubble icon (Notes/Assistant).
*   *Keyboard shortcuts:* `Cmd+/` for Assistant, `Cmd+Shift+H` for Notes.

**2. Adjusting the Text Size**
*   If the text feels too small or too large, click the **"Aa"** button in the top-right drawer menu.
*   Clicking it cycles through three sizes: Small, Default, and Large. 
*   The app remembers your choice.

**3. Highlights, Notes & Citations**
*   **To highlight:** Drag your cursor/finger across text (or tap once for a word, twice for a sentence, three times for a paragraph).
*   **To add a note:** Tap an existing highlight.
*   **Bookmarks:** Tap the top margin of the page. A physical red ribbon will drop down.
*   **Academic Tools:** When you open a highlight in the Notes drawer, you'll see a **"Verified"** checkbox and a **"Cite this"** button (which copies a BibTeX citation to your clipboard).

**4. The AI Companion (The Seminar)**
*   **Interacting with Highlights:** Highlight a word or sentence and click the **"Ask"** button on the colour bar. The Assistant will open with the text already attached. You can also tap an *existing* highlight and switch to the Assistant tab—it will know exactly what you are looking at.
*   **Weight Matching:** Ask a short question like *"What does this word mean?"* and it will give a brief, direct answer. Ask a deeper question like *"Why did Camus write this?"* and it will act like a tutor.

**5. The Librarian (Web Search)**
*   Ask it something outside the book (e.g., *"Search the web for how the Beats connect to Camus"*).
*   It will show a "Searching the web..." prompt. It will **only** state facts found in the search results, and it will admit what the sources *don't* say, rather than inventing a connection.

**6. Sharing & Exporting**
*   At the bottom of any AI response, you'll see a **"Share"** button (on mobile) or a **"Copy"** button (on desktop).
*   Use this to send the AI's synthesis straight into your notes app (Workflowy, Obsidian, Apple Notes, etc.).

**7. Cross-Device Sync**
*   Open the book on your phone, turn a few pages, and wait 10 seconds.
*   Open the same book on your Mac. It should automatically jump to where you left off.

### 🔍 What to look for (The Feedback we need)
*   Does it feel like a book, or does it feel like a website?
*   Is the text size comfortable?
*   Does dragging to highlight feel natural?
*   Is the AI's answer the right length, or does it lecture you?
*   What breaks?
